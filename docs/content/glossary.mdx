---
title: Glossary
description: The terms the app and these docs use for pods (multisig accounts), signers and proposals.
---

## Address

A 32-byte identifier on Sui that can own coins and objects. A pod’s address is derived from its signers’ public keys, their weights and the threshold, so a different setup gives a different address. See [Why the address depends on the setup](/guide/how-a-multisig-works#why-the-address-depends-on-the-setup).

## Combined signature

The single signature that the app builds from the signers’ individual signatures when you execute a proposal. Sui checks it against the pod’s address. The [Signature Analyzer](/guide/tools#signature-analyzer) shows what one contains.

## Digest

The unique ID of a transaction, computed from its bytes. Each proposal is identified by the digest of its transaction, and the app’s proposal links use it.

## Epoch

A period of time on a Sui network. A transaction can name a last epoch it may run in; after that epoch ends, the proposal shows as expired and cannot execute.

## Execution

Submitting a proposal’s transaction to the network with the combined signature, once it has enough weight. See [Execution](/guide/proposals#execution).

## Gas

The fee a Sui transaction pays to run. A proposal’s gas is paid by the pod itself, from its balance on that network.

## Invitation

How a signer joins a pod. Each signer accepts or rejects it by signing a message with their wallet; your own keys accept automatically when you create the pod.

## Network

A Sui network, such as mainnet or testnet. A pod and its address are the same on every network, but each proposal belongs to one network, and balances differ between networks. See [Pods and networks](/guide/how-a-multisig-works#pods-and-networks) and [Networks](/guide/networks).

## Pod

A multisig account: one Sui address controlled by several signers together. See [What a pod is](/guide/how-a-multisig-works#what-a-pod-is).

## Proposal

A transaction from the pod that is waiting for signatures, stored together with the signatures collected for it. See [How proposals work](/guide/proposals).

## Proposal status

Where a proposal stands: Pending, Ready, Expired, Executed, Failed or Cancelled. See [Statuses](/guide/proposals#statuses).

## Proposer

Anyone who creates a proposal. Signers can propose, and a pod can also have [external proposers](/guide/external-proposers): addresses that can propose but not sign.

## Public key

The public half of a wallet’s key pair. A signer is added to a pod by their public key; the app accepts Ed25519, Secp256k1 and Secp256r1 keys. The app can find the key from an address once its wallet has signed in to the app, or once the address has sent a transaction. See [Signers and public keys](/guide/how-a-multisig-works#signers-and-public-keys).

## Signature

Proof, made in a wallet, that the holder of a key approved a specific transaction or message. The app never sees your private key, only the signatures your wallet makes.

## Signed message

A message, not a transaction, that you sign in your wallet. Signing in, answering an invitation, adding or removing an external proposer and cancelling a proposal each need one. See [What needs your signature](/security/trust-model#what-needs-your-signature).

## Signer

One of the public keys that control a pod, each with a weight. See [Signers and public keys](/guide/how-a-multisig-works#signers-and-public-keys).

## Threshold

The total weight of signatures a proposal needs before it can execute. It is a whole number from 1 up to the sum of all signers’ weights. See [Weights and the threshold](/guide/how-a-multisig-works#weights-and-the-threshold).

## Wallet

The software that holds your private key and makes your signatures. The app asks for signatures through the Sui wallet standard from whichever [wallet you connect](/guide/wallets), and the wallet shows its own view of what you sign. Signing in with a wallet registers its public key with the service, so other people can add your address as a signer. See [Check what you sign](/security/trust-model#check-what-you-sign).

## Weight

How much a signer’s signature counts toward the threshold, a whole number from 1 to 255. See [A worked example](/guide/how-a-multisig-works#a-worked-example).
